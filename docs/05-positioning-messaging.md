# 05 — Positioning & Messaging

*Copy **intent** and direction throughout — not final marketing copy. Everything here is written to
survive translation into Greek without going stiff, which rules out most English wordplay.*

---

## 5.1 Positioning statement

> **Fluux is certified point-of-use water filtration for Cyprus — engineered to a measurable
> standard, installed and serviced by a local dealer you can call.**

Every clause is load-bearing:

- **"point-of-use"** — honest scope. Not whole-house, not softening. Prevents the overpromise
  identified in [03](03-cyprus-water-context.md) §3.3.
- **"certified"** — the NSF claims are the strongest differentiator against a market where many
  competitors assert quality without third-party validation. *(Subject to verification —
  [02](02-product-matrix.md) §2.1.)*
- **"engineered to a measurable standard"** — premium as precision, not decoration.
- **"installed and serviced by a local dealer you can call"** — answers the two biggest dealer-model
  objections (*who installs this?*, *what happens when it breaks?*) inside the positioning itself,
  and turns the dealer network from a logistical fact into a proof point.

**What it deliberately omits:** any claim about limescale in appliances, and any claim to be a
Cypriot company. Both would be false, and both would fail at first contact.

## 5.2 Three messaging pillars

### Pillar 1 — Certified, not claimed

*The proposition:* independently tested and certified performance, in a category full of
unverifiable assertions.

*Carries:* NSF/ANSI 42, 53 and 401 certifications; stated micron ratings; published capacity and
flow; the microplastics claim if it survives verification.

*Leads for:* commercial buyers, spec-literate homeowners, English locale.

*Watch:* every certification claim is currently unverified. This pillar is the most powerful and
the most legally exposed. Nothing ships until Fluux confirms it in writing.

### Pillar 2 — Built for Cyprus water

*The proposition:* Cyprus water has a specific character — very hard, largely desalinated and then
deliberately remineralised, chlorinated for distribution. Fluux is specified against that reality,
not against a generic average.

*Carries:* the district and supply-source content; the desalination-remineralisation explainer; the
free water test, which makes the abstract personal.

*Leads for:* homeowners, Greek locale. This is the pillar that differentiates against imported
brands marketing identical copy in every country.

*Watch:* requires the primary WDD data pass to be credible rather than merely asserted.

### Pillar 3 — A person, not a portal

*The proposition:* no cart and no checkout is a **feature**. Water treatment depends on your
water, your plumbing and your usage. A local dealer tests, specifies, installs and services.

*Carries:* the dealer network; the free water test; the locator; named local businesses; response
commitments.

*Leads for:* both audiences — and it is the pillar that converts the absence of e-commerce from an
apparent limitation into the reason to trust the recommendation.

*Watch:* only credible if the dealer network genuinely covers the districts and responds. The
messaging is hostage to operational reality — see [08](08-lead-engine.md) §8.5.

## 5.4 Messaging cut by audience

### Homeowner — what leads

**Lead with cost and certainty, in that order.**

1. **The bottled water comparison.** €200–400 a year *(Tier B — [03](03-cyprus-water-context.md))*,
   plus the hauling, the storage, the plastic. This is the arithmetic they are already doing.
2. **The free water test.** Removes the decision. They are not buying; they are finding out.
3. **The dealer, named and local.** A person in their district, not a call centre.
4. **Certification, as reassurance rather than specification.** "Independently certified" — not the
   standard numbers, which mean nothing to this audience.

**Do not lead with** micron ratings, flow rates, or the product range. A homeowner who meets nine
SKUs before they meet the problem will leave.

### Commercial — what leads

**Lead with specification and continuity.**

1. **Capacity, flow rate and change interval** — the operating numbers.
2. **Everpure and 3M Aqua-Pure compatibility.** For an existing installed base this is the entire
   pitch: same heads, same fitting, different cartridge.
3. **Service continuity.** Who supplies, how fast, what happens when a cartridge fails on a Friday
   in August.
4. **Documentation.** Downloadable spec sheets and certification records — often required for food
   safety audit and HACCP compliance.

**Do not lead with** emotional framing, family imagery, or bottled-water cost. A hotel's F&B
manager is protecting equipment and passing audits.

## 5.5 Tone of voice

**Premium here means engineered precision and clarity — not luxury decoration.** Fluux should read
like a well-written technical document: confident, specific, unhurried, never salesy. Closer to a
good instrument manufacturer than a lifestyle brand.

### Five rules

1. **Specific over superlative.** A number beats an adjective. If there is no number yet, say less.
2. **Say what it does not do.** Scope honesty is the strongest trust signal available to a brand
   nobody in Cyprus has heard of.
3. **Short sentences. One idea each.** Also the single most effective thing you can do for
   translation quality.
4. **Name the human.** "Your dealer will test your water" — not "water testing is available".
5. **No fear-selling.** The market is already anxious about water. Amplifying that is cheap,
   ages badly, and reads as untrustworthy to exactly the sceptical buyer we need.

### We say this / not this

**1 — Certification**

> ✅ *"Certified to NSF/ANSI 42, 53 and 401. The test results are published; the certificate numbers
> are on every spec sheet."*
>
> ❌ *"The ultimate in water purity technology."*

*Why:* the first is checkable and survives translation intact. The second is a superlative that
means nothing, translates into stiff Greek marketing-speak, and invites the question it cannot
answer — *certified by whom?*

**2 — Limescale**

> ✅ *"Cyprus water is very hard, and desalination does not change that — minerals are added back
> after treatment. Fluux improves the water at your tap. It will not descale your boiler; ask your
> dealer what will."*
>
> ❌ *"Protect your home and appliances from damaging limescale."*

*Why:* the first is true, demonstrates expertise, and earns trust precisely by declining the sale.
The second is the claim every competitor makes, and for a point-of-use cartridge it is false —
it would collapse at the water test.

**3 — The dealer model**

> ✅ *"Tell us about your water and we'll put you in touch with your nearest installer. They'll test
> it and quote. One call, and you choose whether to go ahead."*
>
> ❌ *"Submit your details and a representative will be in touch shortly."*

*Why:* the first sets expectations precisely, and pre-empts the "am I about to be cold-called"
objection by describing exactly what happens. The second is the sentence every visitor has learned
to distrust, and translates into even blander Greek.

### Translation rules

The tone survives Greek only if the English is built for it:

- **No idioms, no puns, no wordplay.** They either translate literally into nonsense or force the
  translator to invent, and the brands diverge.
- **No noun stacks.** "Water filtration system installation service" has no natural Greek form.
- **Sentence case.** Title Case does not exist in Greek.
- **Write the CTA verb-first and short.** Long English CTAs become very long Greek buttons —
  see [04](04-bilingual-architecture.md) §4.8.
- **Brief the translator on voice, not just words.** Supply this document. A translator working
  without it defaults to formal commercial Greek, which is a different, stiffer brand.
- **Greek `καθαρό νερό` ("clean water") is close to a cliché in this category.** Ask the Greek
  copywriter for something with more specificity.
