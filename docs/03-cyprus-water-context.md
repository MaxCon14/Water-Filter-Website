# 03 — Cyprus Water Context

*The site's persuasion engine is local water reality, not generic filtration marketing. This
document is the content foundation for the water-problems hub, the district pages and most of the
knowledge base.*

---

## 3.1 Source health — read first

The brief asked us to verify conditions against the **Water Development Department** and the
**district water boards**. **We could not reach them.** All government domains were blocked in the
research environment.

That leaves us with three tiers of evidence, and it is important to be honest about which is which,
because the weakest tier is the one carrying the most persuasive numbers:

| Tier | Source type | Examples | Trust |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Government / peer-reviewed / major press | Cyprus Mail, World Bank presentation, EWRA journal | Cite with date |
| **B** | Independent commercial, no filtration interest | Estatefy (property), Worldometer | Cite, flag |
| **C** | **Competitor filtration marketing** | filter.cy, waterfilternet.com, Spirit Water, altlab.cy | **Do not publish** |

**Every hardness figure available to us is Tier C** — it comes from companies selling water
filters, who have a direct commercial interest in the numbers being alarming. Fluux publishing a
competitor's hardness statistic as fact would be both a credibility risk and, if wrong, a
regulatory one.

**Recommendation:** commission a **primary data pass** in discovery — pull the WDD's published
water quality reports and the district water boards' own analyses. It is perhaps two days of work,
it de-risks every claim on the site, and it produces genuinely proprietary content that no
competitor currently has. This is the single highest-leverage content investment in the plan.

## 3.2 What we believe is true

### Hardness

| Claim | Figure | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cyprus average hardness | ~341 mg/l CaCO₃, classified "very hard" | WaterGrade | C |
| Typical range | 250–350 ppm | Competitor marketing | C |
| Broader range | 250–400 ppm, some >400 mg/l TDS | Competitor marketing | C |

For orientation: >180 ppm is conventionally "very hard". Cyprus is comfortably above that on every
figure available. **The direction of the claim is almost certainly right; the precise numbers are
not ours to publish.**

### Supply and desalination

| Claim | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Desalination supplies ~**80%** of drinkable water | Cyprus Mail, March 2026 | A |
| Desalination supplies ~**70%** | Older reporting | A (dated) |
| Desalination introduced 1997; RO is the main technology | EWRA / press | A |
| 5 large municipal plants + ~24 smaller units | Press / ResearchGate | B |
| Cyprus is water-stressed: <1,700 m³ per inhabitant per year | UN via search summary | B |

Use the 80% figure **with its date attached** ("as of March 2026"), because it has moved and will
move again.

### The counterintuitive fact worth building content around

**Minerals are deliberately added back after desalination.** From September 1999, magnesium
sulphate was introduced in post-treatment alongside lime and carbon dioxide, to stabilise the water
and prevent corrosion of the distribution network.

This is the most useful thing in this document. The consumer assumption — *"we desalinate now, so
the water must be soft"* — is wrong, and correcting it does three jobs at once: it explains why
limescale persists, it demonstrates genuine expertise, and **no Cyprus competitor is currently
leading with it.**

Proposed article: *"Cyprus desalinates 80% of its drinking water. So why is your kettle still
furred?"* — see [13 — SEO & content](13-seo-content-strategy.md).

The €200–400 figure is the most useful number in this document for conversion purposes, and it

**This is the comparison the buyer is already running in their head**, and the site should meet
them there rather than arguing about micron ratings. But we cannot complete the comparison
honestly, because **we do not have the Fluux running cost** — that requires the replacement

[07 — Page blueprints](07-page-blueprints.md) §7.2. It must show the arithmetic rather than
asserting a saving — a stated "save €X" is a claim the visitor must trust, whereas visible working
is a claim they can check, and checkable claims convert better with exactly the sceptical,
value-conscious buyer this market contains.

### On problem 6 — the honesty constraint

Limescale destroying appliances is **the** Cypriot household water grievance, and it is the most
tempting content angle on this list. It is also the one Fluux cannot fully answer: a point-of-use
under-sink cartridge does nothing for the solar water heater on the roof or the washing machine in
the utility room.

**Recommendation: address limescale as education, not as a product claim.** Write the definitive
guide to limescale in Cyprus — it will rank, it serves the audience genuinely, and it builds
authority. But route the *product* conversation to what Fluux actually does: drinking water quality
and point-of-use scale inhibition.

Overpromising here fails twice. It fails at the free water test, when the dealer has to explain in
person that the product does not solve the problem the website implied. And it fails at review
time. Neither is recoverable in a market this small, where the incumbents have been trading on
reputation for decades.

If Fluux does have a whole-house or softening product for Cyprus, this constraint disappears and
problem 6 becomes P0 — which is why it is blocking question #4.

## 3.4 District-level content — recommended, but not for the stated reason

### The honest assessment

The brief asks whether a district page pattern is a genuine SEO asset or thin-content padding. On
the evidence available, **the water-chemistry case for district pages is weak.**

Reported hardness by district *(all Tier C — a competitor's help centre)*:

| District | Reported hardness |
|---|---|
| Nicosia | 280–350 ppm |
| Limassol | 250–320 ppm |
| Larnaca | 260–340 ppm |
| Paphos | 300–400+ ppm |
| Famagusta | Not found |

**These ranges overlap almost entirely.** Four pages saying "the water here is very hard, roughly
300 ppm" in four different wrappers is exactly the thin content the brief is right to be wary of,
and Google is good at recognising it.

### But build them anyway — differentiated on something real

District pages earn their place if they carry information that **genuinely differs by district**.
Three things do:

1. **Which dealers cover this district**, with names, coverage and response commitment. This is
   real, unique, useful, and impossible for a national competitor to replicate.
2. **Supply source** — desalinated, borehole, reservoir, or mixed. This *does* vary meaningfully,
   it explains why the water tastes different in different places, and it is a genuine expertise
   signal. Requires the WDD data pass in §3.1.
3. **Local installation context** — apartment vs. villa stock, typical plumbing, whether solar
   water heaters dominate, new-build density for the developer audience.

**Recommendation: build five district pages as P1, structured as *dealer + supply source + local
context*, with hardness as a supporting detail rather than the headline.** They then function as
local landing pages for "water filter Limassol"-type queries — commercially valuable, genuinely
differentiated, and defensible.

**Do not build sub-district or city-level pages.** That is where this pattern tips into padding,
and five districts is already the natural granularity of both the water boards and the dealer
network.

**Dependency:** district pages are only viable once dealer coverage data exists. That makes them
hostage to the dealer-data collection risk in [15 — Delivery](15-delivery-roadmap.md). If dealer
data slips, ship the district pages late rather than shipping them empty.

## 3.5 Seasonal variation

Cyprus has a pronounced dry season, tourist-season demand peaks in the coastal districts, and
episodic drought driving reservoir levels and desalination reliance.

We found **no verified data** on seasonal variation in delivered water quality.
