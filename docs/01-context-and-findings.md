# 01 — Context & Research Findings

## 1.1 Fixed context

These are given. The plan does not re-litigate them.

- **Brand:** Fluux — water filtration systems.
- **Market:** Cyprus — Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos and Famagusta districts.
- **Languages:** English and Greek, both first-class. Not a translation afterthought.
- **Business model:** lead generation feeding a dealer/installer network. No cart, no checkout, no
  online price. Every conversion is a qualified enquiry handed to a dealer.
- **Audiences, equal weight:** homeowners and commercial buyers.

**Brand assets:** `[CLIENT INPUT REQUIRED]` — no logo, palette, photography or brand guideline was
supplied. [11 — Design direction](11-design-direction.md) is written as direction-setting rather
than as an extension of an existing system, and assumes we are defining the identity, not
inheriting one.

**Competitors:** researched and proposed below, since none were supplied.

## 1.2 What Fluux appears to be

Research indicates **Fluux is a brand of Microfilter Co., a South Korean filtration
manufacturer** (`fluux.co.kr`), distributed internationally through catering-equipment and water-
treatment resellers in the UK, UAE, Oman and the US.

This matters for three reasons the brief does not anticipate:

1. Fluux is **not a Cyprus-native brand**. The site is launching an imported Korean manufacturer
   into a market where the established players trade heavily on longevity and local presence
   (Camelot claims 46 years; Karma claims operation since 1999). Provenance needs a deliberate
   answer — see [14 — Trust & objections](14-trust-objections-analytics.md).
2. The range is **built around compatibility with Everpure and 3M Aqua-Pure heads** — an installed
   base that already exists in Cypriot hotels, restaurants and cafés. That is a genuine
   go-to-market advantage and the site should exploit it.
3. There is an **existing international spec vocabulary** (NSF/ANSI certifications, LPM, litre
   capacities) that the Cyprus site can inherit rather than invent — once Fluux confirms it.

`[CLIENT INPUT REQUIRED]` Confirm the Fluux–Microfilter relationship and whether Fluux Cyprus is a
distributor, a licensee, or a brand operation, and whether Korean manufacture is a story to tell or
to keep quiet.

## 1.3 Six findings that reshaped the brief

### Finding 1 — The product naming in the brief does not match what exists

| Brief says | Research found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Fluux FX — 10", 15", 17" | **FX-10, FX-15, FX-17** (plus `S` variants) | Broadly confirmed |
| Fluux **FHF2** | **HF2**-10S, HF2-15S, HF2-17S | Name differs — likely "HF2" |
| Fluux **HFS** | No such line found. An **H-series** exists: H17-SD3, H17-CS2, H17-PS2 | **Unresolved** |

The H-series found is organised by **media type at a single size**, not by size across a line. The
naming grammar appears to be `{series}{size}-{media}{stage}`.

This is the highest-priority open question. See [02](02-product-matrix.md) §2.2.

### Finding 2 — CORRECTED: the size numbers *are* inches

**This finding was originally wrong and is retained here with its correction, because
the plan and the selector tool were both built on it.**

Originally we concluded that 10/15/17 could not be housing lengths, reasoning that FX-15
and FX-17 share a flow rate (9.4 LPM) and so length could not be the variable. The client
subsequently supplied a photograph of the three FX cartridges side by side. They are
plainly three different physical lengths, in the ratio ~0.6 : 0.85 : 1.0, which matches
10 : 15 : 17 almost exactly.

**What was right:** length drives **capacity**, not flow. FX-15 and FX-17 do run at the
same rate — the label confirms 2.5 GPM / 9.5 LPM — so the larger cartridge buys time
between changes, not performance.

**What was wrong, and what it cost:** under-sink clearance is a genuine fitting
constraint. A 17-inch cartridge needs seventeen inches of clear cabinet height. The
selector tool was written to *avoid* asking about space, on the explicit reasoning that
space was irrelevant — which would have recommended cartridges that do not physically fit.
That has been corrected in [09](09-selector-tool.md) §9.2 and the copy in
[02](02-product-matrix.md) §2.4.

*Ref: assumption A-4 in [99 — Assumptions](99-assumptions.md), which was flagged as the
single assumption most worth testing first. It was, and it failed.*

### Finding 3 — These are cartridges, not standalone systems

The range is repeatedly described as replacement cartridges for **Everpure QL3B, i2000, MC2, ESO7,
MH2, H-300, H-104 and 3M Aqua-Pure** heads. Some retail listings bundle a head ("Includes Head").

Consequences the brief does not cover:

- There are **two lead types, not one**: *replacement* (has a compatible head, needs cartridges,
  possibly no installer at all) and *new install* (needs head, fitting and commissioning).
  Different quote, different dealer skill, different form. See [08](08-lead-engine.md) §8.4.
- The real revenue is **recurring consumable replacement**, not the one-off install. The site
  should capture replacement-schedule intent — a reminder opt-in is a low-friction conversion that
  the brief's inventory misses entirely.
- Competing on an **installed base of Everpure heads** in Cypriot hospitality is a sharper
  commercial wedge than competing on domestic filtration, where incumbents are entrenched.

### Finding 4 — The framing is multi-axis, not a ladder

The brief asks whether this is good/better/best or three parallel answers. Neither, exactly. There
are three axes:

- **Series / technology** — parallel, not ranked. FX is ultrafiltration at 0.1 µm; HF2 is carbon
  block at 0.5 µm with scale inhibition. Finer is not simply better; they answer different
  problems.
- **Size (10/15/17)** — a genuine good/better/best capacity ladder *within* a series.
- **Media suffix (SD / CS / PS / S)** — a functional choice: sediment, carbon, polyphosphate scale
  inhibitor, or scale-inhibited variant.

The IA must express all three without exposing a matrix. Recommendation in
[02](02-product-matrix.md) §2.5.

### Finding 5 — Desalination has not removed the limescale problem

Desalination supplies roughly **70–80%** of Cyprus's drinking water (sources disagree; see
[03](03-cyprus-water-context.md)). But minerals — magnesium sulphate, lime, carbon dioxide — are
deliberately **added back** after desalination to stabilise the water and protect the distribution
network.

So the intuitive consumer assumption ("we desalinate now, so the water must be soft") is wrong, and
correcting it is a genuinely useful piece of content that competitors are not leading with.

### Finding 6 — The Cyprus market is crowded, and the incumbent playbook is already visible

Identified competitors: **Karma Water** (Nicosia, since 1999, domestic + commercial + government),
**Camelot** (Limassol/Nicosia, claims 46 years, free delivery and installation, free water testing
before and after each filter change), **BWT Barrier Cyprus**, **Q Water Cyprus**, **Aquaphor
Cyprus**, plus content-led retail sites `filter.cy`, `waterfilternet.com`, `filtercy.com`, and
project-focused players `altlab.cy` and Spirit Water.

Two observations that shape the plan:

- **The free water test is the established conversion mechanic in this market**, not the quote
  request. Camelot leads with it. Fluux should match it and make it the primary homeowner CTA —
  see [08](08-lead-engine.md) §8.2.
- **Incumbents already own the generic content** ("is tap water safe to drink in Cyprus", "water
  hardness in Cyprus"). Competing head-on for those terms is expensive. See
  [13](13-seo-content-strategy.md) for where we recommend attacking instead.

## 1.4 Research limitations — read before trusting anything here

The research environment blocked all direct page fetching. `fluux.co.kr`, every distributor site
(filterflair.co.uk, uvwater.biz, aadems.com, ceonline.co.uk), Amazon, Wikipedia and the Cyprus
Water Development Department were all unreachable.

**Everything in this plan derived from the web came through search-result summaries**, not from
reading the source pages. That means:

- Specification figures are **secondary-source and unverified against the manufacturer**. They are
  good enough to design an information architecture around. They are **not** good enough to publish,
  print on a spec sheet, or make a legal claim with.
- Some Cyprus water figures come from **competitors' own marketing pages**, which have a commercial
  interest in the numbers being alarming. These are flagged individually in
  [03](03-cyprus-water-context.md).
- The Water Development Department and district water boards were **not** reached. Every claim the
  brief asked us to verify against them remains **unverified**.

**The exception:** Greek typeface support in [11](11-design-direction.md) was verified directly
against Google Fonts' `METADATA.pb` subset declarations over an allowed route. Those findings are
hard evidence, not assertion.

Full list in [99 — Assumptions](99-assumptions.md).
